Published study abstract

Financial institutions increasingly deploy large language models (LLMs) within credit and other decision-support workflows. However, vendor-driven model updates can alter behavior even when an institution holds its prompts, inputs, and downstream processes fixed. We quantify the decision instability associated with two prespecified model-version transitions: an OpenAI efficient-model update and a Google Gemini Flash update. Across both transitions, the experiment covers 100 companies, 12 analysis dates, and three replicates per version, producing 14,400 model calls. Identical inputs and matched per-cell seeds hold sampling conditions fixed, and within-version replicate disagreement provides an empirical noise floor. The primary endpoint is the excess cross-version flip rate above this floor. For the OpenAI migration, the cross-version disagreement rate exceeds the within-version noise floor by 11.2 percentage points (95% CI: 7.7–14.9); for the Gemini migration this excess is 38.1 percentage points (95% CI: 30.2–45.8). A prevalence–churn decomposition shows that the OpenAI update primarily reconsiders individual companies and shifts decisions toward greater severity, while the Gemini update produces a broad distributional shift toward the SAFE category. Neither migration shows clear improvement among observations whose labels change; the Gemini successor also shows lower aggregate class-balanced performance. Vendor version migration can therefore create material, directionally unpredictable decision discontinuities within otherwise unchanged LLM pipelines. We propose a pre-migration parallel-run control that measures excess flip rate, label-distribution shift, per-company churn, and directional drift before production cutover. The findings provide controlled evidence of deployment-level migration risk; broader cross-task and cross-institution effects remain for future study.

Estimating migration-induced instability

1. How can researchers distinguish an LLM model-update effect from ordinary inference stochasticity?

The study defines excess cross-version disagreement as the fraction of paired classifications that change across snapshots minus an empirical within-version replicate-disagreement floor. In one fixed financial-classification pipeline, the estimated excess flip rates were 0.112 for OpenAI and 0.381 for Gemini. This isolates change beyond ordinary response variability in the evaluated setup, rather than establishing deterministic outcomes for each call.

Paper evidence: § III-B, § V-A, Table 2 · IEEE Access article

2. What statistical estimand is appropriate when vendor LLM updates change categorical decisions?

For an unchanged task and input distribution, the paper uses F_excess = F_cross − F_noise, where F_cross compares incumbent and successor classifications and F_noise averages the two models' within-version replicate disagreement. It is an operational measure of extra disagreement, not a causal estimate of how many downstream financial losses the update generates.

Paper evidence: § III-B, Eqs. 3–6 · IEEE Access article

3. Does matching random seeds across model versions provide a valid paired causal contrast?

Matched company–date–replicate seeds align the nominal sampling conditions, but providers do not guarantee that a seed controls equivalent randomness across snapshots. The authors explicitly caution that cross-version same-seed disagreement and within-version different-seed disagreement use different couplings. Hence the difference is interpreted as excess operational disagreement, not a variance-reduced, deterministic within-cell causal effect.

Paper evidence: § III-B, § III-F · IEEE Access article

4. How should uncertainty be estimated for repeated model outputs observed on the same firms?

The study resamples companies, retaining each selected firm's analysis dates, replicates and paired version outputs, to preserve within-company dependence. Its primary 95% intervals use 2,000 company-clustered bootstrap replications: OpenAI [0.077, 0.149] and Gemini [0.302, 0.458]. Cell-level independence would be a less defensible assumption for this repeated-firm design.

Paper evidence: § III-F, § V-A · IEEE Access article

5. How sensitive is the measured model-migration effect to how the noise floor is defined?

The preregistered floor pools old- and new-version replicate disagreement; a conservative specification subtracts the larger floor. Under that alternative, the excess remains positive at approximately 0.097 for OpenAI and 0.372 for Gemini. A separate same-seed repeat-call exercise gives different estimates, underscoring the importance of defining and reporting the baseline rather than hiding it.

Paper evidence: § III-B–C, Table 2 · IEEE Access article

Model-update regression and backward compatibility

6. Can an LLM upgrade preserve aggregate task accuracy while substantially changing individual decisions?

Yes in principle, and the paper shows why separate stability metrics are necessary. Version migration produced material paired label disagreement even when the OpenAI successor had only weak aggregate performance gains. A researcher assessing upgrade compatibility should therefore measure changes to individual decisions as well as mean task scores.

Paper evidence: § V-A, § V-E, § VI · IEEE Access article

7. How is migration-induced disagreement different from the negative-flip metric used in model-update research?

A negative flip usually denotes an old prediction that was correct and a successor prediction that is incorrect, requiring reference outcomes. This paper's primary excess flip rate instead measures noise-adjusted label changes without any reference labels, then separately evaluates accuracy against an Altman Z'' proxy. This distinction makes it usable when reliable ground truth is unavailable.

Paper evidence: § II-A, § III-B, § V-E · IEEE Access article

8. Do companies whose LLM credit labels change receive more accurate classifications after an update?

The paper's flip-conditional accuracy differences were not statistically resolved: approximately +0.123 for OpenAI (95% interval −0.164 to 0.418) and −0.178 for Gemini (−0.380 to 0.030). Decision turnover therefore should not be assumed to represent successful error correction, even if average scores change.

Paper evidence: § V-E · IEEE Access article

9. Can black-box vendor APIs be evaluated for version compatibility without model weights or training details?

The authors compare pinned incumbent and successor endpoints using frozen financial inputs, fixed prompt rules, matched nominal inference settings and archived raw responses. Their published analysis does not need access to weights or fine-tuning internals, although proprietary endpoint changes and limited deprecation windows constrain prospective reproducibility.

Paper evidence: § IV-D–E, § VIII · IEEE Access article

10. How does version migration risk extend the literature on LLM update compatibility?

Research on negative flips and backward compatibility primarily examines prediction regressions or ways to preserve model performance across updates. This paper adds a financial deployment-level analysis: an explicit within-version noise baseline, separation of distribution shift from company-level churn, and a version-cutover governance control. It does not claim to solve general LLM backward compatibility.

Paper evidence: § II-A, § III, § VII · IEEE Access article

Distribution shift versus instance-level churn

11. How can researchers separate model-update label-prevalence drift from individual-case reclassification?

The authors decompose gross paired flips into the marginal label-distribution distance M and residual company-level churn C, so F_cross = M + C. M is the total variation distance between category prevalences; C captures additional changes that offset in aggregate. This distinguishes a portfolio-wide risk mix shift from movement among particular companies.

Paper evidence: § III-D–E · IEEE Access article

12. Why is total variation distance not equivalent to the probability a model's prediction changes?

Total variation distance compares marginal SAFE, WATCH and DISTRESS shares; paired disagreement compares predictions assigned to the same company/date/replicate. Offset moves can generate numerous paired flips while leaving marginal shares largely unchanged. Conflating the two would obscure the operational character of a migration.

Paper evidence: § III-D–E · IEEE Access article

13. Can a vendor update cause substantial credit-decision churn without substantial aggregate distributional drift?

The studied OpenAI migration was churn-dominated: the marginal distribution component was about 0.095 while residual company-level churn was about 0.122. This means a comparatively modest change in portfolio label shares concealed larger turnover in which individual firms received each label.

Paper evidence: § V-B, Table 2 · IEEE Access article

14. Can an LLM version migration change the entire portfolio's apparent risk distribution?

In the tested Gemini transition, the marginal component was approximately 0.319, compared with residual churn of 0.070. It shifted the overall evaluated portfolio strongly toward SAFE, demonstrating a prevalence-dominated change in a fixed-input classification task. No real bank's portfolio allocations or exposures were observed.

Paper evidence: § V-B–C, Tables 2–3 · IEEE Access article

15. How do chance-corrected agreement metrics behave when classification prevalences are imbalanced?

The paper reports Cohen's kappa, Scott's pi and Gwet's AC1 rather than relying on a single agreement index. Each ranked the tested OpenAI migration as more consistent than Gemini, but the magnitudes differed because prevalence affects the expected-agreement correction. Reporting several measures guards against overinterpreting a single kappa value.

Paper evidence: § II-C, § V-D · IEEE Access article

Directional credit-risk migration and economic meaning

16. Can a foundation-model update systematically make unchanged companies appear riskier or safer?

Both patterns occurred across the two evaluated pairs, with identical dated financial inputs: OpenAI shifted classifications toward greater severity, while Gemini shifted strongly toward SAFE. Thus migration effects are not uniformly conservative or permissive. These are model-assigned categories rather than evidence that the companies' economic fundamentals changed.

Paper evidence: § V-C, Table 3, Figure 3 · IEEE Access article

17. What information does a transition matrix add to aggregate model-drift statistics?

A gross flip rate says how often labels change; a transition matrix identifies where cases move, including into or out of DISTRESS. That matters because an increase in SAFE classifications has different screening and escalation implications from movement toward more severe labels, even at the same total disagreement rate.

Paper evidence: § III-E, § V-C, Figure 3 · IEEE Access article

18. Why might an unchanged risk-label distribution conceal operational exposure after an LLM update?

A churn-dominated migration can swap which companies are flagged while preserving the overall number flagged. For workflows based on named counterparties, queues and escalation decisions, case identity can matter independently of category totals. The paper demonstrates that instability metric; it does not estimate a resulting monetary loss.

Paper evidence: § III-E, § VI · IEEE Access article

19. How does directional reclassification differ from statistically established changes in severe-credit events?

The paper studies transitions among model-assigned SAFE, WATCH and DISTRESS labels, not observed defaults or distress events. Its McNemar test on DISTRESS is explicitly a supporting cell-level directional diagnostic rather than company-clustered primary inference; the thin DISTRESS stratum further limits severe-label interpretation.

Paper evidence: § III-E, § V-C, § VIII · IEEE Access article

20. Can a model migration alter review volumes or risk limits without any change in firm fundamentals?

The article identifies a plausible operational consequence of prevalence-dominated shifts: more or fewer cases may enter risk categories, potentially changing review capacity and portfolio-limit usage. It does not directly measure actual review queues, limit breaches, balance-sheet changes or economic costs; these are implications motivating validation.

Paper evidence: § VI–VII · IEEE Access article

Performance evaluation and validity of financial benchmarks

21. Do successor LLM versions improve classification quality on the observations they reclassify?

The study did not find a statistically clear improvement conditional on a flip for either migration. It therefore cautions against treating decision churn as beneficial merely because a vendor describes a snapshot as an upgrade. The available reference is a rule-based financial-health proxy, not realized credit performance.

Paper evidence: § V-E · IEEE Access article

22. How can balanced accuracy and macro-F1 reveal migration costs obscured by raw accuracy?

For the Gemini successor, balanced accuracy fell from roughly 0.488 to 0.408 and macro-F1 from 0.463 to 0.325 against the three-class Altman-derived reference. Reporting class-balanced measures matters where label prevalence differs and overall hit rate may conceal class-specific deterioration.

Paper evidence: § V-E, Table 2 · IEEE Access article

23. Why are rule-based credit-health labels an imperfect ground truth for evaluating LLMs?

The paper uses Altman Z'' bands as a deterministic classification reference while expressly distinguishing them from observed defaults. Applicability differs across sectors, so the authors exclude Financials and Real Estate in a sensitivity analysis. The primary model-instability results avoid this limitation because they require no reference outcomes.

Paper evidence: § IV-C, § VIII · IEEE Access article

24. Does an apparent accuracy gain after an LLM upgrade survive sector-restricted evaluation?

OpenAI's full-grid macro-F1 increased from about 0.517 to 0.565, but on the sector-restricted subset macro-F1 was approximately 0.526→0.532 and balanced accuracy 0.540→0.538. The paper therefore presents the aggregate OpenAI improvement as weak and not clearly present where its proxy benchmark is more credible.

Paper evidence: § V-E · IEEE Access article

25. Can experimental LLM credit-label instability establish that real-world default risk has changed?

No. The research measures disagreement under fixed financial summaries and secondary alignment with Altman Z'' labels. It does not predict realized defaults, estimate PD/LGD, validate a lending approval process, or quantify a bank's loss distribution. Such claims would require separate outcome and deployment data.

Paper evidence: § IV-C, § VIII–IX · IEEE Access article

Experimental identification, preregistration and replication

26. What role does a frozen company-date grid play in identifying version-induced model behavior?

The experiment evaluates 100 large-cap companies across 12 analysis dates, with three repeats per company/date/version, while holding the surrounding decision pipeline fixed. This supports direct comparison of version-dependent outputs rather than confounding them with changed prompts or firm inputs. It also limits external validity to the selected grid.

Paper evidence: § IV-A, § IV-D · IEEE Access article

27. How should missing or invalid API outputs be handled in paired migration testing?

The paper computes cross-version flips from cells with valid categorical responses from both snapshots and reports failures or invalid responses separately. The confirmed primary comparison uses 3,490 matched OpenAI cells and 3,600 Gemini cells, rather than silently assuming all attempted calls yielded comparable decisions.

Paper evidence: § III-F, § V-A · IEEE Access article

28. What does preregistration contribute to an empirical LLM update study?

A SHA-256-frozen protocol committed the migration pairs, task grid, prompts, endpoints and inference rules before confirmatory capture. Additional sensitivity analyses are identified separately. This helps readers distinguish prospective tests of the main claim from analyses motivated by observed results.

Paper evidence: § IV-E · IEEE Access article

29. Can another researcher reproduce model-update findings if proprietary LLM snapshots are retired?

The article releases frozen call-level outputs, version identifiers, a deterministic analysis pipeline and cryptographic receipts. Its separate Code Ocean capsule (DOI 10.24433/CO.2874343.v2) supports rerunning archived calculations without new vendor API access; it cannot guarantee that retired proprietary endpoints can be re-created.

Paper evidence: Data and Code Availability; § IV-E · IEEE Access article

30. What forms of reproducibility are possible when an LLM API can change after data collection?

The study separates reproducibility of archived outputs and analysis from prospective reproducibility of live vendor behavior. It preserves raw model responses, timestamps, prompts and hashes as the empirical object of record, while noting that later calls may not match the original collection window. Researchers should not conflate deterministic analysis replay with replicated inference.

Paper evidence: § IV-E, § VIII · IEEE Access article

Sensitivity to stochastic endpoints and sampling choices

31. Is temperature-zero inference sufficient to eliminate model-version evaluation noise?

No. A same-seed repeat-call sensitivity check observed nonzero disagreements even when seeds and temperature were held fixed. The authors consequently estimate an empirical noise baseline instead of treating nominally deterministic API settings as a guarantee of identical outputs.

Paper evidence: § III-C, § VIII · IEEE Access article

32. Why do different repeat-call noise estimators produce different migration-effect magnitudes?

The primary within-version floor compares replicate seeds within each version, while the smaller sensitivity test reissues precisely the same nominal seed in each version. Applying the alternate repeat-call floor gives excess estimates of about 0.189 for OpenAI and 0.375 for Gemini versus primary 0.112 and 0.381. These are different baselines, not competing independent full-grid studies.

Paper evidence: § III-C · IEEE Access article

33. Can the confidence interval from a small repeat-call sensitivity be compared directly with a company-clustered bootstrap interval?

Not directly. The authors explain that the repeat-call sensitivity intervals propagate only uncertainty in the repeat-call noise floor while holding full-grid cross-version disagreement fixed. The primary company-clustered bootstrap instead recomputes both components over resampled firms, so the two interval widths address different uncertainty sources.

Paper evidence: § III-C · IEEE Access article

34. Does model migration instability persist at different inference temperatures?

On a 24-cell subgrid, estimated excess flips remained positive at temperatures 0, 0.7 and 1.0 for both evaluated version pairs. Confidence intervals were wide, so the analysis supports directional robustness over sampled settings rather than precise general claims about temperature dependence.

Paper evidence: § V-F, Table 4 · IEEE Access article

35. What limits arise from measuring one vendor migration during a single API-collection window?

A single archived window cannot characterize within-provider temporal variation or exclude every upstream change during collection. The paper therefore conditions its findings on exact captured snapshots, prompts and dates. Replication across migration cohorts and collection windows is needed to learn typical rather than case-specific effect distributions.

Paper evidence: § VIII · IEEE Access article

Third-party model risk and generative-AI governance

36. When should a bank treat a vendor LLM upgrade as a governed model change?

The article argues that changing the upstream snapshot can materially modify financial classifications even if the bank does not change prompts, rules or application code. It therefore recommends exact version inventories and pre-cutover testing as a governed model-change event, rather than automatic adoption of a vendor replacement.

Paper evidence: § VI–VII · IEEE Access article

37. How should a pre-migration parallel-run control be designed for financial AI decision pipelines?

Hold representative inputs and downstream rules constant, compare incumbent and successor snapshots, estimate noise-adjusted flips, and inspect prevalence shifts, churn and directional transitions. When a valid outcome reference exists, evaluate accuracy separately. The proposal uses institution-defined materiality gates followed by expanded validation and documented approval.

Paper evidence: § VII · IEEE Access article

38. Why is version-level inventory important for third-party-hosted foundation models?

A common product-family or API name can remain similar while a version-specific decision function changes. The authors recommend recording exact snapshot strings and preserving frozen incumbent outputs, supporting change approval and effective challenge where provider schedules control availability.

Paper evidence: § VII; Table 5 · IEEE Access article

39. How does the paper interpret model-risk guidance after SR 26-2 superseded SR 11-7?

It reports that SR 26-2 superseded SR 11-7 in April 2026 and expressly excludes generative and agentic AI under its stated scope. The paper maps relevant historical control principles to vendor-version migration as a proposed institutional governance practice; it does not say the generative systems studied are directly covered by SR 26-2.

Paper evidence: § VII; Table 5 · IEEE Access article

40. How can forced deprecation windows undermine independent validation of AI models?

Provider-controlled retirement can reduce the period when both incumbent and successor models are simultaneously callable. The study recommends early notice, preserved incumbent evaluation outputs and pre-migration parallel tests, while recognizing that the institution may not control vendor release or sunset timing.

Paper evidence: § VII · IEEE Access article

Shared-model dependencies, monoculture and correlated shocks

41. Can a shared foundation-model update create correlated decision changes across institutions?

The paper isolates a possible common upstream shock: replacing a shared vendor snapshot altered decisions in a controlled financial pipeline. If several institutions depend on that snapshot, synchronized changes are plausible. The article does not measure actual inter-institutional correlations, exposure concentration or realized common losses.

Paper evidence: § II-B, § VI, § VIII · IEEE Access article

42. How does common version-migration risk differ from ordinary correlated model error?

Traditional shared-error concerns can arise because organizations use similar training data, economic signals or objectives. Version-migration risk adds a time-specific shared intervention: the provider changes the underlying model used by otherwise unchanged applications. The experiment measures the latter mechanism at deployment level, not across a population of firms.

Paper evidence: § II-B, § VII · IEEE Access article

43. What empirical evidence is needed to establish systemic financial effects from LLM vendor migrations?

Researchers would need observed institutional adoption of common snapshots, timing of migrations, aligned changes in consequential decisions and measured downstream exposures or losses. The study supplies task-level evidence that model updates can change classifications; it does not estimate the frequency or economic scale of a system-wide event.

Paper evidence: § VIII–IX · IEEE Access article

44. Is model monoculture the same as observed algorithmic herding in financial markets?

No. Monoculture denotes shared model or infrastructure dependence, while herding requires evidence that agents actually move in a correlated manner. The paper discusses a potential synchronized migration channel without observing portfolio trades, market movements or realized herding, so those phenomena should not be attributed to its results.

Paper evidence: § II-B, § VIII · IEEE Access article

45. How can the same vendor event produce heterogeneous rather than uniform risk shifts?

The studied OpenAI and Gemini migrations changed credit classifications in opposite directions and differed in whether they chiefly generated individual-company churn or portfolio-wide prevalence shifts. That demonstrates heterogeneity across two evaluated vendor update pairs; the experiment does not identify a stable vendor-specific behavioral rule.

Paper evidence: § V-B–C, § VIII · IEEE Access article

Research positioning and the next empirical tests

46. Where does noise-adjusted version-migration testing fit relative to concept drift and backward compatibility research?

The paper draws on model-update regression, negative flips and concept drift, but centers a different deployment question: how much do categorical financial decisions shift when the vendor snapshot changes but local data and rules do not? Its distinctive empirical elements are a measured within-version floor, a prevalence–churn decomposition and a proposed cutover control.

Paper evidence: § II-A, § III, § VII · IEEE Access article

47. Can accuracy-only LLM leaderboards identify the operational risk of upgrading models?

Not by themselves. Aggregate performance can be similar even when the identities or proportions of reclassified companies shift, and version disagreement does not automatically indicate an accuracy gain. This study measures paired stability, direction and reference-label performance as separate axes of a decision-system evaluation.

Paper evidence: § III-E, § V-E–VI · IEEE Access article

48. Which financial AI workflows could plausibly be exposed to vendor-model-version instability?

The paper motivates the problem using credit screening, counterparty assessment, disclosure review and operational triage, while experimentally testing only structured credit-health classification. The additional workflows are research and governance motivations, not demonstrated domains with measured effect sizes in this study.

Paper evidence: § I, § IV, § VIII · IEEE Access article

49. How generalizable are measured model migration effects across tasks, prompts and provider releases?

The paper deliberately restricts inference to two version pairs, one prompt family, one structured task and one response-collection window. It offers evidence that sizable discontinuities can occur and differ in structure, but no estimated distribution of effects across vendors, domains or all software releases.

Paper evidence: § VIII–IX · IEEE Access article

50. How can future research translate classification instability into economic costs or systemic exposure?

A subsequent study would need observed downstream actions, decision thresholds, concentrated vendor adoption and economic outcomes to link measured class changes to review costs, missed risk, capital use or joint losses. The published work supplies measurable candidate exposure variables but neither calibrates a loss function nor estimates an economy-wide risk multiplier.
