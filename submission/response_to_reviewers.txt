Reviewer #1, Concern #1

Reviewer comment:

1. Redefine or defend the excess-flip estimand

The within-version rates compare different replicate seeds, whereas Fcross compares the same
nominal seed across versions. Because seed behavior is provider and version specific, these
quantities need not share a common coupling.

Author response:

  - We agree that Fwithin and Fcross use different constructions and need not share a common
    coupling.
  - We therefore no longer interpret Fexcess as a variance-reduced within-cell contrast or as the
    causal share of decisions changed by migration.
  - As an alternative sensitivity check, we estimated same-seed repeat-call disagreement on a
    stratified subgrid.
  - The repeat-call floors are 0.028 for OpenAI and 0.014 for Gemini.
  - Applying these alternative noise-floor estimates to the full-grid cross-version disagreement
    rates gives excess disagreement of 0.189 [0.147, 0.206] for OpenAI and 0.375 [0.339, 0.385] for
    Gemini.
  - The substantive conclusion is unchanged.

Author action:

  - Section III-C, "What the Two Baselines Measure, and a Repeat-Call Sensitivity," was added.
  - The section now:
      - explicitly distinguishes the construction of Fwithin and Fcross;
      - narrows the interpretation of Fexcess;
      - reports a same-seed repeat-call sensitivity analysis on a stratified subgrid;
      - clearly states that the resulting repeat-call floor is applied as an alternative noise-floor
        estimate to the full-grid cross-version disagreement rate; and
      - retains the preregistered endpoint as primary.


Reviewer #1, Concern #2

Reviewer comment:

2. Separate distributional distance from per-call migration flips:

If estimated with independent replicate draws, the proposed difference has a defensible
categorical distribution-distance interpretation. That is different from the probability that a
specific production decision will flip after cutover. Explain which operational question each
statistic answers and avoid treating them as interchangeable.

Author response:

  - We agree that these quantities answer different operational questions.
  - TVD measures movement in the aggregate label distribution.
  - Fcross measures matched decision disagreement across versions.
  - We also clarify that TVD equals the marginal component M of the prevalence-churn decomposition.

Author action:

  - Section III-D, "Distribution Distance Is Not Per-Call Flip Probability" was added.
  - The new section:
      - formally distinguishes TVD from Fcross;
      - reports both quantities side by side for both migration pairs;
      - explains their different operational interpretations; and
      - links TVD explicitly to the marginal component M.
